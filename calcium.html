The Calcium Bottleneck

Calcium travels in the cell walls and nowhere else. That means the Casparian strip stops it — everywhere except the few millimetres of root that have not built the strip yet. Run the scenarios and watch where it actually ends up.

Why doubling the calcium in the soil does almost nothing

Uptake is limited by how much unsuberised root tip the plant currently has, not by how much calcium is out there. Behind the tip the endodermis has closed the apoplast, and calcium — which never gets a symplastic ride, because cytosolic Ca²⁺ is held at nanomolar concentrations on purpose — simply cannot cross. Run Extra calcium fertiliser and then Hot & dry: the fertiliser barely moves the fruit, and the drought, which stops new root growth, wrecks it.

The second bottleneck is downstream. Once in the xylem, calcium goes where the water goes. A leaf transpires hard; a waxy fruit hardly transpires at all, and is fed mostly by root pressure at night. So the leaves take the great majority, and because Ca²⁺ is not phloem-mobile, none of it ever comes back. Blossom-end rot is the fruit losing that competition.

Why the inset shows two panels

Same root, two ages. Behind the tip the strip is built and the apoplastic route dead-ends. At the tip it has not been laid down yet, and calcium walks straight in.

Passage cells

Even in mature root, a few endodermal cells stay unsuberised opposite the xylem poles. Real, but a trickle compared with the tip.

The grower's mistake

Reaching for calcium spray or fertiliser when the plant needed steady water and undamaged roots.
